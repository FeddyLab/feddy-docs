title: Identify users
description: Tell Feddy who's logged in, so you see their name in the inbox, their history follows them across devices, and replies can reach them by email.
---

By the end of this page, conversations from a logged-in user will show their name and details in your inbox, and their history will follow them onto every device they sign in on.

You don't have to do this. Feddy works from the first install: until you identify someone, each device is treated as its own anonymous visitor, with an ID Feddy stores on the device. Identifying users is what turns "an iPhone running 2.3.1" into "Ada, on the Pro plan".

## When to call it

Call `identify` right after the user logs in, and again whenever something you send about them changes. It's safe to call as often as you like, even on every launch; each call updates what's stored.

```swift
Feddy.identify(
    userId: user.id,
    email: user.email,
    attributes: ["plan": user.plan, "is_member": user.isMember, "renews_at": user.renewsAt]
)
```

```js
Feddy.identify({
  userId: user.id,
  email: user.email,
  attributes: { plan: user.plan, is_member: user.isMember },
})
```

`userId` is your own ID for the user, the one your backend uses. Everything else is optional.

## What happens when you do

- The anonymous visitor on this device becomes that user. Their email, name, picture, and details now show in the inbox.
- If Feddy already knows this `userId` from another device, the two records merge into one. The conversations from this device move over, and the user sees a single history everywhere they log in.
- The details you send replace the previous set as a whole. Send everything you want to keep on every call; leave a key out and it's removed. Email, name, and picture are the exception: they stay if you don't send them.

## Details about the user

Alongside the ID, you can send facts that help you answer faster: their plan, whether they're a paying member, when their trial ends, how long they've had an account. Feddy calls these **attributes**. They're simple key-value pairs; values can be text, numbers, true/false, or dates written like `2026-09-22T10:00:00Z`.

<Note>
Attributes are for facts about the person. Device model, OS version, app version, and language are collected by the SDK and shown with every conversation already, so there's no need to send those.
</Note>

There are limits, but hitting one never causes an error. Anything over a limit is quietly dropped, so a mistake in your integration can't stop a user from writing in.

| Limit | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Keys per contact | 20 |
| Key length | 40 characters |
| Value length | 255 characters as text |
| Total size | 8 KB |
| Value types | text, number, true/false, date as `2026-09-22T10:00:00Z` |

Nested objects and lists are dropped too. Keep it flat.

Once you've sent a key, it appears under **Settings → Data attributes** in the dashboard, where Feddy has worked out whether it's text, a number, or a date. From there you can give it a friendlier label for the inbox, show it as a column in the conversation list, or make it something you can filter by.

## Email

Pass `email` when you have it. If your project has a verified email domain, replies are also emailed to the user, so they hear back even if they never reopen the app. Without a verified domain the email is stored but nothing is sent.

If you don't pass an email and your project has a verified domain, the widget may ask the user for one after their first message. It only asks once per device.
